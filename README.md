# Pointers

MLA 9 in-text citation, quotation, and page layout, practiced on a page of your own paper. A companion to [Core Elements](https://github.com/DrMarsden/core-elements).

An in-text citation is a pointer. It sends the reader to one entry in the Works Cited list and to the exact place in that source, so it gives the first word of the entry and a location. Pointers teaches that idea and everything around it: where the punctuation goes, how quotations are carried in a sentence, how poems, plays, and long passages are set, and how the first page of a paper is laid out. It is one web page. There is nothing to install and no server.

**Use it:** https://drmarsden.github.io/pointers/

Or download `index.html` from this repository and open it in any current browser.

## What it does

- **Starts with a guide.** Twelve short steps cover the pointer idea, author and page, more than one author, titles as pointers, names, punctuation around quotations, poems and plays, block quotations, changing a quotation, titles in your own sentences, quoting well, and the first page. The guide's examples come from invented sources kept separate from the exercises.
- **Works on your paper.** Each exercise shows a short Works Cited list, the original passage, and a sentence of the student's paper. Marked spots in the sentence (a citation, a line break, an ellipsis, a title, a mark of punctuation) open the possible forms; every wrong form carries its own short correction.
- **Lays out a first page.** The last exercise starts from Word's defaults (Aptos 11, 1.08 spacing with 8 points after each paragraph, a page-number-only header in Word's header font, a bold title, justified text) and redraws the page as the student fixes each setting.
- **Marks course rules.** Rules MLA leaves open but this course sets are tagged *This course*: Times New Roman at 12 points, how to introduce a quotation, and avoiding floating quotations. The typeface rule is one line at the top of the page's script (`COURSE`); set `on:false` to accept any readable typeface.
- **Issues a completion slip** with the student's name, score, date, and a check code, to paste into a course site. An exercise counts once it is right, however many tries it takes.

Every source is invented for practice, except two lines each of Poe's "The City in the Sea" and *Hamlet*. The rules follow the Purdue OWL and the MLA Style Center, and nothing else; where a rule rests on a Style Center post written for the eighth edition, or on the instructor's reading of the Handbook, the instructor's key notes it.

## Limits

- It teaches MLA 9 in-text citation and paper format. For works-cited entries, use Core Elements.
- The checking is only as good as its keys. If you find a form it marks wrong that MLA would accept, please write.
- The completion slip carries a check code that stops casual editing of a slip, but anyone who reads this page's source can forge one. Treat it as a convenience, not as security.

## How it was made

Pointers was written in October 2026 by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, in conversation with Steve Marsden, who supplied the purpose, the list of what students get wrong, his own teaching on quotation, and the rule that only the Purdue OWL and the MLA's own pages count as authorities, and who judged the results. He did not write the code.

## Privacy

Progress is kept in your browser's own storage on your device. Nothing is sent to a server. The page requests its typefaces from Google Fonts.
